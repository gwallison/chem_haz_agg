    ### Tier Level Rule for GHS H-codes and Reasons for Separation

    **The Rule:** A hazard code is assigned to **Tier 1** if its GHS Category contains a "1" or a "2" OR if its Signal Word is "Danger". If neither of these conditions is met, the code is assigned to **Tier 2**.

    **Reasons for Separation:** The Globally Harmonized System (GHS) classifies health and environmental hazards to harmonize chemical regulations globally. While the GHS does not explicitly use this specific "Tier 1 / Tier 2" grouping, applying this rule effectively separates the hazards based on severity. The classifications captured in **Tier 1** (Categories 1 and 2, and the "Danger" signal word) represent the most acute and severe health and environmental hazards—such as chemicals that are fatal, toxic, carcinogenic, mutagenic, or cause severe burns. Conversely, the classifications captured in **Tier 2** (Categories 3 through 5, and the "Warning" signal word) generally denote lower-severity risks, such as substances that are merely "harmful," cause mild irritation, or pose lower-level environmental risks.
